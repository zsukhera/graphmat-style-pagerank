Observations:
- Load balancing is the biggest lever on the real graphs: dynamic beats static by up to
  2.46x on LiveJournal and 1.54x on Enron, in line with their 2.5-6.5x static imbalance.
- rmat-20 is balanced (1.07) and static ~= dynamic up to 8 threads (all P-cores). At 16
  threads static drops to 5.87x while dynamic reaches 8.95x (ratio 1.53): that jump is the
  E-cores, not the graph. Same reason ell_scalar/simd (static schedule) fall back at 16.
- SIMD alone: -1% to +10% over the scalar ELLPACK control (typically 5-8%; rmat-20
  at 1 thread is the one case where it is 1% slower). The layout
  change gives more than SIMD at low thread counts (Enron 2.3x at 1 thread).
- Best end to end: 8.95x (dynamic, rmat-20, 16 thr); combined 8.07x on rmat-20, 6.35x on
  LiveJournal. Scaling flattens from 8 to 16 threads for every variant: P->E cores plus
  memory bandwidth.
- email-Enron at 16 threads is slower than at 8 for combined (0.14 vs 0.06 ms): an
  iteration is ~0.1 ms, so fork/join and E-core stragglers dominate. Small-graph case.

"Fewer instructions, fewer stalls"?
- Fewer instructions: YES. ELLPACK cuts instructions 32% vs CSR (500 -> 341 M/iter), AVX2
  a further 26% (341 -> 250 M). Total -50% vs static CSR.
- Fewer stalls: NO. Stall cycles are flat (331-354 M at 1 thread) and ~75-80% of them are
  L3-miss stalls. Cache misses are the same (~12.5 M/iter) for every non-serial variant. The kernel is
  memory-latency bound; SIMD removes instructions, not misses, so time improves only
  6-10% (cycles 543 -> 513 M) and IPC falls (0.63 -> 0.49).
- At 16 threads total cycles roughly double vs 1 thread with identical instructions: the
  threads spend cycles waiting on memory, and L3-miss stalls rise to ~90% of stalls.
